# R4: Learned world models / generative simulators (round 1)

Researched 2026-10-26. Scope: block 4 "From simulation to reality", final slide "what's next". The team bases its talk on vision (camera input).
Grades: A = independent replication; B = one paper with measured numbers; C = vendor or product claim, or demo; D = blog or anecdote.
Flags: -coi (conflict of interest), -1run (single run), -indirect, -abstract or -snippet (read only the abstract or a snippet), -old (more than 12 months old and presented as current).

## Summary

- **The idea is old, but the scale is new.** Ha and Schmidhuber (2018) trained a car-racing and Doom agent inside a learned "dream". DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., Nature, published 2 Apr 2025) mastered more than 150 tasks with one recipe. It was the first algorithm to collect Minecraft diamonds from scratch, with no human data. Its input is a 64x64 camera image plus inventory vectors.
- **V-JEPA 2 is confirmed** (arXiv 2506.09985, 11 Jun 2025). Its encoder has about 1B parameters (ViT-g) and was trained on more than 1M hours of internet video. The robot-planning variant (V-JEPA 2-AC) adds a predictor of about 300M parameters, trained on less than 62 h of unlabeled DROID robot video. Zero-shot pick-and-place on Franka arms in two new labs succeeded 80% of the time for a cup and 65% for a box, versus 15% and 10% for Octo. Each condition has only 10 trials.
- **Genie 2** (Dec 2024) stays consistent for up to about 1 minute, with most clips 10-20 s. **Genie 3** (5 Aug 2025) runs at 720p and 24 fps in real time, stays consistent for "a few minutes", and remembers about 1 minute. DeepMind ran its SIMA agent inside these worlds. SIMA 2 (13 Nov 2025) also *self-improved* inside unseen Genie 3 worlds, but DeepMind published no numbers for this, only demo videos.
- **Industry generative simulators are mostly demos or vendor claims, with no published measurement of real-world benefit.** Examples: Wayve GAIA-1 (Sep 2023; 9B parameters; 4,700 h of London driving) and GAIA-2 (Mar 2025); NVIDIA Cosmos (Jan 2025; about 20M h of raw video; 4B-14B models); the Waymo World Model, built on Genie 3 (6 Feb 2026; camera and lidar); Tesla's "neural world simulator" (Oct 2025; secondary sources only); the 1X World Model (Jun 2025; used to *evaluate* policies). The Cosmos paper itself says it has no empirical downstream policy results.
- **Drone link verified: SkyJEPA exists** (arXiv 2606.23444, 22 Jun 2026; Rao, Zhang, Balestriero, LeCun, Loianno). But it **uses state, not vision**: the inputs are position, velocity, attitude, angular rate and motor forces, with no camera. It is trained only in a randomized hand-built physics simulator, then transfers zero-shot to a real 1.3 kg quadrotor outdoors. On a real circle trajectory its position error was 0.24 m versus 0.39 m for the baseline.
- **Best vision-plus-drone link: "Dream to Fly"** (Romero, ..., Scaramuzza, UZH, the same lab as Swift; arXiv 2501.14377; ICRA 2026). DreamerV3 flies through gates using only camera pixels, at up to 9 m/s on a real quadrotor. However, the real flights used a hardware-in-the-loop setup with *rendered* images, not a physical camera.
- **Counter-evidence is strong.** Video generators copy the nearest training example instead of learning physics laws (Kang et al., ICML 2025). Their physical understanding is "severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism" (Physics-IQ, Jan 2025). The Cosmos authors report "object impermanence" and "violation of gravity". The V-JEPA 2 authors name long-horizon error accumulation and camera sensitivity as limits. On Meta's own physics benchmarks, humans score 85-95% and models score far lower. Ha and Schmidhuber's agent learned to *exploit* bugs in its dream.
- **Positive real-world evidence exists but is thin.** World-Gymnast (Feb 2026) claims that RL inside a video world model beats a software-simulator baseline on a real Bridge robot by "up to 2x" (abstract only). FlightDiffusion (Sep 2025) reports no statistically significant gap between simulation and reality for a drone policy trained on generated FPV video. No independent replications were found, so nothing reaches grade A.

## Plain-language explanations (first-year level)

- **World model.** A world model is a neural network that has watched lots of video and learned to answer "if I do X, what will I see next?". Think of it as a video game engine that nobody programmed: it learned the "rules" by watching. A robot can "imagine" several moves inside it and pick the best one before moving for real. (Meta's analogy: you know a tossed ball falls back down, and you'd be surprised if it hovered or turned into an apple.)
- **Training "in a dream" (Ha and Schmidhuber; Dreamer).** First the AI learns a mini-simulator from experience. Then it practises millions of times inside that mini-simulator, which is cheap and safe, like a pilot rehearsing a flight in their head. Dreamer repeats this loop: real experience improves the dream, and the dream improves the behaviour. The catch: if the dream has bugs, the agent may learn to exploit the bugs instead of the real skill.
- **JEPA (LeCun; I-JEPA; V-JEPA; V-JEPA 2).** Most video generators try to draw every pixel of the future. JEPA instead predicts a compact *summary* (an "embedding") of what comes next. It is like predicting "the ball will be on the floor" without painting every leaf on the tree behind it. This is cheaper, and it ignores unpredictable detail that doesn't matter for acting.
- **Generative interactive world model (Genie 3, GAIA, Cosmos, Waymo).** These are AI video generators you can *steer*. You press "forward" or change the weather, and the next frames are generated live. Companies want them as an "infinite test track", for example to create a tornado or an elephant on the road that real cars almost never see. Today they look very realistic, but nobody has published proof that a car or robot trained this way drives or works better in reality.
- **Sim-to-real gap.** A hand-built simulator is like a driving school on a closed course: clean, but not like real traffic. A policy that is perfect in simulation can fail in reality because the light, physics or sensors differ slightly. Learned world models try to close this gap by learning "reality" directly from real video. They bring a new problem, though: hallucinated physics.
- **Hallucinated physics and compounding error.** A generative model can make objects vanish, float or pass through walls (Cosmos's own authors report this). Each predicted frame is built on the previous *predicted* frame, so small mistakes snowball, like a game of broken telephone. That is why most of these models stay consistent for only seconds to minutes.
- **SkyJEPA, the drone example.** It applies the JEPA idea to a quadrotor. It learns to predict the drone's flight from numbers (position, speed, tilt), not from camera images. The drone then "imagines" hundreds of possible motor commands 100 times per second and picks the best one. It was trained only in a physics simulator and still flew well outdoors. Caveat for our slide: it does **not** use a camera, and it still needed a hand-built simulator for data.
- **Dream to Fly, the vision drone example.** It comes from the same Zurich lab as Swift. A Dreamer-style world model learns to fly through gates using only camera pixels. Caveat: the "real" flights used computer-rendered camera images, so it is not yet a full test with a real camera.
