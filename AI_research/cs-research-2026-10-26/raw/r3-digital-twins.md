# R3: Digital twins / simulators rebuilt from real photos and video (round 1)

Researcher run date: 2026-10-26. Scope: block 4 "From simulation to reality" (KSE CS101). Team rule: vision only (camera), environments from photos/video, not laser scans.

## Summary

- **The pipeline in one line:** photos or a phone video, then camera positions via structure-from-motion (COLMAP), then a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) "twin" that renders new camera views fast enough (100+ fps) to train a drone's camera-based policy inside it. SOUS VIDE (Stanford, 2024/25) builds its scene from a **2-3 minute handheld walk-through video**, renders at **up to 130 fps**, and reports zero-shot real flights (105 hardware flights). [B]
- **Why splats and not NeRF:** on the same benchmark, 3DGS renders at **134 fps against 0.06 fps** for Mip-NeRF360 and trains in about **41 min against 48 h** (Kerbl et al. 2023, Table 1). [B, foundation, 2023]
- **Feed-forward models remove the slow step.** VGGT (CVPR 2025) estimates cameras and 3D points for about 10 images in **~0.2 s, against ~15 s for COLMAP-based** pipelines on an H100 (Table 1). DUSt3R and MASt3R (2024) started this "no calibration needed" line. [B]
- **Drone splat-simulator papers are real and checked.** All four arXiv IDs in the brief resolve. One title in the brief is wrong: 2406.15149 is "Gaussian Splatting to Real World Flight Navigation Transfer with Liquid Networks" (MIT, Rus lab), not a "dynamics-augmented GS simulator". Its scene came from about **430 iPhone photos, with a 15-minute splat training run**. [B]
- **The 2026 relightable-3DGS paper (2602.07101) needs two corrections.** It says "up to 10 m/s", not ">10 m/s". Its scenes were captured with **LiDAR + RTK plus a stereo fisheye camera**. The policy uses only one RGB camera, but the twin is not photo-only, so it **conflicts with the team rule**. Its own ablation measures that baked-in lighting matters: real success went from **18/30 to 26/30** when the twin's lighting was varied, and at twilight from **3/10 to 8/10**. [B, -1run, small n]
- **Measured limits:**
  - Splat twins get worse when the camera moves away from where the photos were taken. On EUVS (ICCV 2025), 3DGS PSNR drops **23-29%** on extrapolated views.
  - Splat twins have no physics. Every robot paper adds a separate collision mesh (Vid2Sim, VR-Robo) or a ground plane (NVIDIA NuRec).
  - SOUS VIDE broke when real objects were removed from the scene: only **1 of 5** fully successful runs, 4 of the 5 completed position only, and 1 failed outright.
- **The idea is going mainstream.** NVIDIA ships photo-to-Isaac-Sim reconstruction (3DGUT/NuRec). Their Aug 2025 workflow uses about 100 photos, then COLMAP, then 3DGUT, then USD. Isaac Sim 6.0 makes it a GA (general-availability) feature [C]. GaussGym (Berkeley, Oct 2025) renders splats at >100,000 sim steps/s from iPhone scans. [B, -abstract]
- **Big-place twins (context only):**
  - Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles: launched May 2023, GA 26 Oct 2023, 2,500+ cities [C].
  - VastGaussian (CVPR 2024) and CityGaussian (ECCV 2024) scale splats to whole city blocks.

## Plain-language explanations (first-year level)

- **Photogrammetry / structure-from-motion (SfM, COLMAP).** Walk around a building taking photos. The software finds the same corner or window in many photos and works backwards, like triangulating a GPS position, to compute where each photo was taken and where those points sit in 3D. The output is a "dot cloud" of the scene plus every camera's position. COLMAP is the standard free tool for this. It placed about 21,000 tourist photos of Rome in about 3 hours.
- **NeRF (Neural Radiance Field).** A neural network learns to answer one question: "at this 3D point, looking from this direction, what color is it and how foggy or solid is it?" To draw a picture, you ask that question millions of times along every pixel's ray. The pictures look excellent but are slow: about 30 seconds per frame in the 2020 paper. A drone simulator needs hundreds of frames per second.
- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS).** Instead of a network, the scene is millions of tiny, soft, colored, semi-transparent blobs ("splats"), like a 3D pointillist painting. The computer adjusts their position, size and color until rendered views match the photos. Drawing blobs is something graphics cards are very fast at: about 134 fps against 0.06 fps for a top NeRF. That speed makes them usable as a training gym for robots.
- **Feed-forward reconstruction (DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT).** The old way (SfM) solves a big puzzle from scratch for every new scene. These models have seen millions of scenes and "just know" the 3D shape when you show them a few photos, the way you can guess a room's layout from two snapshots. VGGT does this in about 0.2 seconds for ~10 images.
- **Digital twin / real-to-sim.** A digital twin is a virtual copy of a real place, built here from a video, so that a robot can practice thousands of times in it. Crashes in the twin cost nothing. The trained "brain" is then copied to the real drone. "Zero-shot" means it flies in reality without any extra practice there.
- **Sim-to-real gap.** The differences between the practice world and reality: lighting, missing or moved objects, wind, the drone's real weight. A splat twin closes the "how things look" gap well, but the other gaps remain. SOUS VIDE adds random wind and mass in training, and the forest paper (D7) varies lighting.
- **What a splat twin cannot do.**
  - Splats are just paint with no solid surface, so a separate invisible "collision shell" (mesh) must be added for crashes and contacts.
  - The scene is frozen in time, so nothing moves.
  - Shadows and sunlight are "painted in".
  - Views far from where the video was shot look blurry or broken, with PSNR falling 23-29% in one benchmark.

Its twin uses LiDAR + RTK in capture, which conflicts with the team's photo-only rule. If you use it, say "the drone flies with one camera, but the map was made with laser + camera".
