Grading: A = peer-reviewed with measured data; B = independent source reporting measured data, or a measured public result confirmed by an independent party; C = vendor or self-report. Flag `-coi` = the source is the company itself (conflict of interest). "Inference" = my reading, not stated by the company.

## Summary: the 5 best examples for slides

1. **TU Delft MAVLab "MonoRace", winner of A2RL 2025 (drone racing, vision only).** This is the cleanest sim-to-real story with numbers. It used one forward camera. The control network was trained with reinforcement learning in a *simplified simulation* with 40–50% domain randomization and transferred zero-shot. Real lap times "closely match" simulated ones. The gate detector was trained on about 3,500 synthetic plus 500 real images ("a little real data"). It reached up to 95.8–100 km/h and beat 3 human world champions. One caveat: it is a university lab, not a company.
2. **Wayve PRISM-1 → GAIA-2 → GAIA-3 (driving, camera only).** One company shows two techniques. PRISM-1 builds a 4D digital twin from camera video only, and GAIA-2/3 are generative world models used to test the driving AI. GAIA-3 (Dec 2025) has 15B parameters, 10x the data of GAIA-2, and covers 9 countries.
3. **Waymo World Model (Feb 2026, built on DeepMind Genie 3).** This generative simulator creates rare events such as a tornado or an elephant on the road, and can turn an ordinary dashcam or phone video into a simulation. The scale contrast works on a slide: about 200 million real autonomous miles against "billions" of virtual miles. Waymo uses lidar too, so present it only as a world-model example.
4. **Figure AI humanoid walking (Mar 2025).** This is textbook domain randomization. Thousands of simulated robots, each with different physics, collected "years" of experience in a few hours, and the policy ran zero-shot on 10 real robots. It is easy for first-year students to grasp. It is not a drone, but it shows the same idea.
5. **DJI Terra 5.0 (Jul 2025) / Pix4D (2026) Gaussian splatting.** The drone photographs a place, and the software builds a photoreal 3D twin. This links block 4 to drones directly. The consumer version students already know is **Google Maps Immersive View** (NeRF from photos, Google Research blog 2023), or the Scaniverse, Polycam and Luma phone apps.
- Honorable mention: **NVIDIA Omniverse NuRec + Isaac Sim 5.0 (Aug 2025)**. It is the "photos → Gaussian splats → simulator" pipeline in one product, and NVIDIA suggests about 100 overlapping photos for a capture. It is vendor-only (C-coi) but very concrete.
- Myth to fix: **Microsoft AirSim was NOT archived in 2022.** In 2022 Microsoft *announced* it would retire AirSim. As of 2026-10-26 the GitHub repo `microsoft/AirSim` has `archived: false` (last push 2026-09-15). The successor **Project AirSim** is maintained by **IAMAI** at `iamaisim/ProjectAirSim` (created 2025-05-13, last push 2026-10-23, 920 stars).
- Avoid on slides: Shield AI, Anduril, The Fourth Law and Swarmer. They publish few technique statements (mostly job posts or press), their claims are C-coi, and the military context goes against the brief's non-operational rule. Mention them at most as "autonomy is used in defense", with no specifics.
