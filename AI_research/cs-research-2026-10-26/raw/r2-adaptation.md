# R2: Closing the sim-to-real gap with little or no real data (vision focus)

Round 1. Researcher R2. Compiled 2026-10-26.
Scope: block 4 of the CS101 talk (Swift drone through-example). Domain randomization and simulators belong to block 2; training methods belong to block 3. Digital twins proper belong to another researcher.

Grades: A = replicated across independent groups; B = one paper with measured numbers; C = vendor or product claim; D = blog or anecdote.

## Summary

1. **Swift needed very little real data.** The fine-tuning used three real flights, about 50 s of flight time. Its dynamics residual model used 800 to 1,000 samples per track. Only the control policy was re-trained; the gate-detection network was frozen. A second round of fine-tuning gave "negligible" gains. Swift's own limitation: perception assumes the scene looks the way it did in training, so a lighting change can break it. (Kaufmann et al., Nature 2023, Methods; grade B.)
2. **Image translation (sim to "real-looking", or real to "canonical sim") works, and the numbers are confirmed in the tables.** RCAN (CVPR 2019, Table 1): 70% zero-shot grasp success on unseen objects using no real grasps. Plain simulation gets 21%, domain randomization alone 33-37%. With 5,000 real grasps RCAN reaches 91%, beating QT-Opt trained on 580,000 real grasps (87%), with under 1% of the real data. QT-Opt fine-tuned on a further 28,000 grasps still wins at 96%, against RCAN's 94% with 28,000.
3. **"Zero real data" often hides real data.** GraspGAN and RL-CycleGAN never use real labels, but their translation networks were trained on 9.4 million and 580,000 real images respectively. RCAN is the cleanest example of truly zero real data in this ladder.
4. **Abstraction is the strongest evidence for drones.** Give the policy something that looks the same in sim and reality (depth from stereo, feature tracks, gate corners, segmentation) instead of raw pixels. Deep Drone Acrobatics: a raw-image policy got 0% success on unseen backgrounds in sim; a feature-track policy got 100%, and transferred to the real drone with no fine-tuning. Driving (Müller 2018): 27% real-world success from images vs 82-100% from segmentation (only 11 trials). Swift itself abstracts images into gate corners plus VIO (visual-inertial odometry).
5. **Generative models (diffusion, video world models) are the 2024-2026 frontier, with promising but small-n numbers.** LucidSim (CoRL 2024) trained a robot dog on generated images. In 10-20 real trials per task it scored 73-100%, against 27-70% for domain randomization. NVIDIA's Cosmos-Transfer2.5 augmentation raised one real-robot policy from 1/30 to 24/30 in new scenes (vendor, -coi). There are critiques: video models' "physical understanding is severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism" (Physics-IQ, 2025).
6. **A little real data mixed with lots of sim data is a well-measured recipe (2025).** Sim-and-real co-training (Maddukuri et al., 2025) raised average real success from 45.3% to 83.2% on 6 tasks, with only 20-50 real demos per task. The best mix was about 99% sim samples per batch, but 99.9% dropped success from 95% to 60%. Matching the sim camera to the real camera mattered: 67% fell to 56%, and 95% to 70%.
7. **Real-to-sim-to-real (RialTo, 2024).** Scan the real scene (about 15 min), collect 15 demos (about 30 min), fine-tune with RL in that copy, then distill back to the real robot. Average success: 91% with pose changes, 77% with distractors, 75% with disturbances. Plain imitation learning from 15 demos scored 10/0/0% on the book-on-shelf task.
8. **Counter-evidence.** CyCADA closes only about half the gap (GTA5 to Cityscapes mIoU 17.9 to 35.4, against 60.3 for an oracle). Pretrained visual encoders transferred zero-shot on navigation (90% vs 10% from scratch) but most manipulation policies "drop to near zero" (Silwal et al. 2023/24). A small 2025 offline study ranked DINOv2-B worst on sim/real domain invariance among 23 encoders. I found no drone paper that uses frozen DINOv2/CLIP features for sim-to-real.

## Plain-language explanations (first-year level)

**The sim-to-real gap.** A simulator is a video game the robot practises in. The game's pictures and physics are never exactly like reality, so a robot that is perfect in the game can fail in the real world. In RCAN's experiments a grasping robot trained only in a plain simulator succeeded 21% of the time in reality. RL-CycleGAN reports the same robot setup at 95% in sim and 21% in reality.

**Image-to-image translation (CycleGAN, GraspGAN, RL-CycleGAN, CyCADA).** A neural network learns to "repaint" simulator pictures so they look like real camera photos, like a photo filter that turns a cartoon into a photo. CycleGAN's trick: if you repaint cartoon to photo and back to cartoon, you should get the original, which keeps the content from changing. The robot then learns from the repainted pictures. The catch: the filter is learned from many real photos (millions for GraspGAN), just not labelled ones.

**RCAN, "sim-to-sim".** Instead of making sim look real, RCAN does the reverse. It learns to turn any messy picture (randomized sim or real) into one clean, simple "canonical" sim picture. Analogy: whatever handwriting you get, first retype it in one standard font, then read it. The robot only ever has to understand the clean font, so it needed zero real grasps to reach 70%.

**Abstraction (what Swift does).** Do not show the robot raw pixels. Show it only the things that look the same in the game and in reality, like a sketch instead of a photo. Swift sees the gates' corner points and an estimate of its own position (VIO, visual-inertial odometry: the camera plus a motion sensor tracking how you moved). Other examples are depth maps from two cameras and feature tracks (dots that follow corners in the image). Because grass colour or lighting are not in the sketch, they cannot confuse the policy. In Deep Drone Acrobatics, a raw-image policy failed completely (0%) on new backgrounds; the "sketch" policy kept 100%.

**Pretrained "foundation" vision encoders (DINOv2, CLIP).** These are big networks already trained on millions of internet images. The hope is that they describe a sim picture and a real picture in the same way, like a well-travelled person who recognises a chair whether it is drawn or photographed. The evidence is mixed: it worked very well for indoor navigation (90% vs 10%), but most simulated-to-real manipulation tasks dropped to near zero. One small 2025 study even found DINOv2 the least sim/real-invariant of 23 encoders.

**Generative AI as a "reality filter" (LucidSim, Cosmos-Transfer, GenAug, ROSIE).** Newer image and video generators (diffusion models, the same family as image-generator apps) can paint endless realistic scenes on top of the simulator's geometry. LucidSim trained a robot dog only on such generated images and beat classic randomization in real tests. Caveat: generators can make pictures that look right but break physics.

**System identification.** Measure the real machine (weight, motor strength, drag) and set the simulator's numbers to match, like tuning a racing game's car settings to match your real car.

**Residual models (Swift's key step).** Fly the real drone a few times, record where reality differs from the simulator's prediction (the "residual" = leftover error), and teach the simulator to add that error. Swift did this with only about 50 s of real flight, then kept training the policy in the corrected simulator. Analogy: a GPS that learns "this map is always 3 m off near the river" and corrects for it.

**Fine-tuning with a little real data, and co-training.** First learn mostly in sim, then practise a little in reality. Or mix sim and real examples in every lesson. In a 2025 study, adding sim data to 20-50 real demos raised success from 45% to 83%. The best mix was mostly sim (about 99%), but not all sim.

**Real-to-sim-to-real (RialTo).** Scan the real room with a phone to build a digital copy. Let the robot practise thousands of times in that copy, including being pushed around, then bring the skill back to the real robot. It is like rehearsing a speech in a replica of the actual hall.
